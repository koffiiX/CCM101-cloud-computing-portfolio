# Mission 3: Multi-Cloud Explorer Reflection

Working on the Multi-Cloud Explorer mission was a great experience because it gave me a clearer picture of how modern cloud platforms actually work in the real world. Before this, it was easy to think that choosing a cloud provider was just about picking whichever name was most popular. However, comparing AWS, Microsoft Azure, and GCP showed me that each platform has its own strengths depending on what a project actually needs.

Out of the three, Google Cloud Platform (GCP) stood out to me the most. I really liked its clean, user-friendly interface and how straightforward it feels to navigate. On top of that, its strong focus on containerized applications with Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) and built-in AI capabilities like Vertex AI makes it feel modern and very developer-focused.

Even though all three providers have their own unique dashboards and feature sets, they still share a lot of fundamental concepts. Each one relies on a global network of regions and availability zones, uses a pay-as-you-go pricing scheme, and offers equivalent core services for compute, storage, networking, and identity management. Learning how these services map to one another made multi-cloud architecture feel much less overwhelming.

Stepping into the role of a Cloud Solutions Consultant made me realize that business requirements always come first. A company shouldn't just pick a provider at random; they need to consider factors like current software licenses, budget constraints, growth potential, and specific technical needs.

Overall, this activity really improved my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio. It added actual research, comparison tables, scenario evaluations, and practical Linux terminal evidence—giving me much more confidence in documenting technical cloud projects.
